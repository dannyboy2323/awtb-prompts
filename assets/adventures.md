> _Every adventure in this document is a production blueprint. It contains the historical event, the hidden truth behind it, the real star character written as a South Park–style literary caricature, the specific comedic and emotional angles to hit, the prison joke suggestion, the life lesson, and the Fun Police subplot. When it's time to write the actual story, this document does most of the work._

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

Each entry contains:

- **Setting:** Year, location, what's happening when Danny and Daniel land
- **The Real History:** The true, little-known, "Bill O'Reilly behind-the-scenes" facts that make the story sing
- **The Star:** The historical figure written as a literary caricature — outrageous, funny, specific, and true
- **Daniel's Role:** Exactly what Daniel does to save the day or change history
- **The Danny Angle:** The emotional/prison connection that grounds the adventure
- **The Prison Joke:** Suggested slang deployment and the exact terrible moment for it
- **The Dad Joke:** (Element 14, Stories 3+) — the corny joke Danny tells in the present-day frame; written in below from `dad-jokes.yaml` (the index of record) by story number; never reused
- **The Fun Police:** The Prosecutor and/or The Hater's subplot
- **The Lesson:** The life lesson this story earns
- **Continuity Notes:** Series threads, callbacks, or foreshadowing

**Ratio:** Stories 1–75 are historical/real events. Stories 76–100 introduce the first wave of fantasy/root beer realms at the ~25% rate the series maintains.

**Consumption status:** Stories 1 and 2 are WRITTEN and PUBLISHED (adventureswiththebull.com). Story 3 is OUTLINED (`story003-outline.md`). Everything else is planned.

### STORY 1 — _CHARGE_ ✅ PUBLISHED

**Setting:** July 1, 1898 — Kettle Hill and San Juan Hill, Cuba. The Battle of San Juan Hill. The Spanish-American War.

**The Real History:** The charge on San Juan Hill — the most famous moment of Teddy Roosevelt's life — was actually a near-disaster. Roosevelt's Rough Riders were pinned down, outnumbered, with no cavalry support. The regulars on their flanks (the Buffalo Soldiers of the 9th and 10th Cavalry — Black regiments who are almost never mentioned in textbook accounts) were doing the heavy lifting and arguably saved the entire charge. TR was the only officer on horseback, making him the most visible target on the battlefield. A Cuban sharpshooter had him in his sights. Twice.

**The Star:** _Teddy Roosevelt_ — rendered as a hurricane in a colonel's uniform. Spectacularly bucktoothed grin visible from 400 yards. Bellowing "BULLY!" at the top of his lungs regardless of what is happening — someone is dying? "BULLY!" The Spanish are retreating? "BULLY!" Daniel accidentally knees him in the ribs? "BULLY!" He is completely, absolutely fearless in the manner of a man who has never once entertained the concept of death applying to him personally. He rides a horse named Texas. He is unhinged in the best possible American way.

**Daniel's Role:** The Cuban sniper's second shot is aimed directly at Roosevelt's head. Daniel, who has been keeping low in the grass, launches out of nowhere and tackles TR off his horse — covering them both in Cuban mud and grass and blood from a nearby casualty. Roosevelt looks at this filthy, panting, grinning eleven-year-old boy and does not know what to say for the only time in his recorded life.

**The Danny Angle:** Prison flashback to Danny being in the SHU, 23 hours a day, alone. He survived by doing push-ups and counting breaths and pretending he was on a hill somewhere with Daniel. The courage it takes to be still when everything in you wants to run.

**The Prison Joke:** At the absolute height of the charge, bullets everywhere, Roosevelt rallying the troops with his massive horse saber raised — Daniel taps Danny on the shoulder and says, _"Hold on, I gotta go send a fax to the warden."_ Roosevelt stares. Danny closes his eyes.

**The Fun Police:** The Hater disguises himself as a Spanish soldier. Falls off the hill three times. Gets trampled by TR's horse Texas. Never seen by Danny or Daniel.

**The Lesson:** Courage is not the absence of fear — it's doing the thing anyway while you're terrified. Danny tells Daniel: _"The bravest people I ever met were scared out of their minds. They just moved forward anyway."_

**Continuity Notes:** The image of Daniel — face marred by dust and blood and sweat, laughing — burns itself into Roosevelt's memory. This is the origin of Story 4.

### STORY 2 — _BIG POPPA_ ✅ PUBLISHED

**Setting:** 1992 — Bed-Stuy, Brooklyn, New York. A block party. Wallace is 20 years old, large as a small building, rapping freestyles for pocket money and a growing neighborhood reputation.

**The Real History:** Christopher Wallace — the Notorious B.I.G. — grew up in the Bedford-Stuyvesant neighborhood of Brooklyn. He was dealing drugs at the same time he was writing lyrics, and the tension between those two lives is the emotional engine of his greatest work. "Big Poppa" wasn't released until 1994 (on _Ready to Die_) but was written and workshopped much earlier. Wallace was known to his friends by many nicknames — "Big," "Chris," "Biggie" — but "Big Poppa" was different. It came from somewhere specific that even biographers argue about.

**The Star:** _Biggie Smalls / Christopher Wallace, age 20_ — rendered as a mountain disguised as a teenager. Enormous, gentle, funny, with a quick-moving brain behind sleepy eyes. He's wearing a Coogi sweater that is doing its absolute best. He raps while eating a beef patty. He is extraordinarily comfortable in his own enormous body. He has the quiet charisma of someone who already knows exactly who he's going to become but isn't showing you yet.

**Daniel's Role:** Daniel, being Daniel, starts calling Danny "Big Poppa" loudly and affectionately on the street — not knowing where they are, just doing the thing he always does. Biggie, leaning against a stoop fifteen feet away, hears it, turns slowly, and something in his eyes clicks. He writes it down immediately on the paper bag he's been using to scratch lyrics. Daniel doesn't notice. The greatest nickname in rap history was coined by an eleven-year-old accidentally calling his dad by a prison-era term of endearment.

**The Danny Angle:** Danny grew up not far from this world. He knows what it looks like when a brilliant kid is standing at a fork in the road and doesn't know it. The flashback is Danny, early in his career, at a similar moment when he could have gone either way. He chose wrong. He sees Biggie and sees himself. Specifically, Danny flashes back to a moment when Danny was founder and CEO of Trustify (the Internet Startup Danny caught his Federal Securities Fraud and Money Laundering case, and 97 month federal prison sentence, over. Danny was Founder and CEO and ultimately took the fall for a lot of malfeasance, some by himself yes, but he also took the fall for others). He and other members of his staff have just realized that their Chief Operating Officer (COO), an older and very well respected Executive and former Federal Prosecutor from the Southeastern District of New York, has been embezzling money from Trustify. They have him on an audio recording admitting to it and a Fairfax County Detective is chomping at the bit for Danny to authorize pressing chargers and going in to arrest the COO. Danny ultimately decides not to press charges and instead allows the COO to resign and retain his reputation and dignity and avoid prison. Danny does not receive the same treatment later when he is the one in the hot seat for his own malfeasance. Danny should have seen this for what it was, a warning to himself not to be like that COO, and to rather walk the line and avoid even being in such a situation in the first place. Unfortunately, back then, he missed this clear warning shot from the Universe.

**The Prison Joke:** Daniel says to BIGGIE "It's store day, and I gots money on my books" which makes everyone stop and crack up laughing, when Danny asks Daniel where he heard that, he shrugs and says "Visitation".

**The Fun Police:** The Prosecutor and the Hater are in a rickshaw in NYC, stuck in traffic trying to get to Brooklyn from Manhattan. Classic. He arrives after Danny and Daniel are already gone. He gets his suit rained on waiting for a cab. Since we failed to give much of an introduction to the villians at all in Story 1 we need to introduce them properly here. Give just enough to explain the concept of the Fun Police and to introduce these two antagonists so the reader understands who they are.

**The Lesson:** Your words have power you don't always see. The things you say casually land somewhere. Danny writes: _"You called me Big Poppa on a street corner and I nearly broke in half. You didn't know. That's the point. You never know."_

**Continuity Notes:** This is one of the series' most emotionally direct letters. The dedication for this story should reference "the boy who renamed me." Jen drives a **green Jeep** (established in the Story 2 outline). Danny lives with his father ("Grandpa") at series start.

### STORY 3 — _I SAVED THE KING_ 📝 OUTLINED (see `story003-outline.md`)

**Setting:** Late 1297 — the Ettrick Forest and the Kirk o' the Forest, Selkirk, Scotland. The raw weeks after Stirling Bridge. William Wallace is about to be knighted and named Guardian of the Kingdom. Robert Bruce, Earl of Carrick, age 23, is in the kirk — the earl tradition says laid the sword on Wallace's shoulder. A royal hunt in the forest. A wild white bull. A Border family from Rule Water who are about to get a new name.

**The Real History:** After Wallace and Moray destroyed an English army at Stirling Bridge (Sept 11, 1297), Wallace was knighted and made Guardian at the Kirk o' the Forest — the church in the great Ettrick hunting forest, where Selkirk now stands (the town's name means "church in the forest"). The knighting earl is most often said to be Robert Bruce, who had already switched sides once that year at Irvine and would switch again before he killed Comyn in a church and crowned himself in 1306. The question nobody in that kirk would say aloud: _who is actually in charge of Scotland?_ Separately — and this is the hinge — the chronicler Hector Boece records the Turnbull origin legend: a giant Borders man, **William of Rule** of Bedrule on the Rule Water, saved Robert Bruce from a charging bull on a hunt by seizing its horns, turning its head, and breaking its neck. Bruce dubbed him **"Turn-e-bull."** The charter is real: Robert I granted William Turnebull the lands of **Philiphaugh — on the edge of Selkirk, a mile from the kirk — on August 15, 1315.** The clan crest is a bull's head sable; the motto is **"I Saved the King."** Tradition dates the bull to ~1315; a Turnbull appears in records twenty years earlier; historians shrug. The series uses the softness: the bull happened in 1297, in the forest, with a boy, and history rounded it off. The Rules (Adam and Thomas) had sworn homage to Edward I on the Ragman Roll in 1296 — in late 1297 they are a family deciding whether to stand back up. The Turnbulls would later be the only clan with a royal bounty on their heads, would found the University of Glasgow (1451), and would produce the giant William Turnbull who, with his black mastiff, challenged the English host to single combat at Halidon Hill in 1333 and died doing it.

**The Boice Connection (locked):** The Turnbull blood is on Danny's **father's** side. Grandpa's mother — Danny's grandmother — was **Helen Trumbull**, Scottish Borders stock; the name was americanized from _Turnbull_ to _Trumbull_ when her great-grandparents immigrated. She told Danny the bull story in her basement when he was Daniel's age. He filed it under things grandmothers say. She and all four of his grandparents are gone; both his parents are living. Grandpa has never told the story and never will.

**The Stars:** _William Wallace_ — a force of nature that hasn't learned it's a force. Enormous; smells of mud and horses and three months of winning; a face carved to be screaming, at rest, which is worse. Privately almost shy — thanks the woman who brings him water, twice. Being fitted for a knighthood like a coat he doesn't believe will fit. _Robert Bruce, Earl of Carrick, 23_ — handsome the way a knife is handsome; fur at the collar; the only comfortable man in the kirk because he's the only one who has already stood on both sides of the war this year and found the footing fine. Speaks French to the bishop's man, Scots to Wallace, Gaelic once to make a point. Has the eyes of a young man who knows he'll be king and thinks it's bad manners to mention it. _Will o' Rule_ — nineteen, six and a half feet, a face like a shovel, a manner so gentle it takes a minute to see that the gentleness is the danger. Owns a black mastiff the size of a hay bale that adopts Daniel on sight.

**Daniel's Role:** The bull comes out of the treeline at Bruce as the kirk empties. Daniel does not run (Cash told him not to). He goes still — then steps _inside_ the arc of the horns the way Master Kim has taught him to step inside a kick, puts both hands on the near horn, and **turns** it. Not stops. Turns. Six hundred pounds of bull goes eight feet left of the future King of Scots and down on its knees, and Will o' Rule arrives at a dead run and finishes it. Bruce, to Will: _"You turned the bull."_ Will looks at Daniel, at the hundred men who didn't see, at the boy who'll be gone by morning — and says, _"Aye, my lord. We did."_ Bruce hears the "we," lets it go, and says: _"Then that's your name. Turn-e-bull."_ History gets the name. Daniel gives it the move.

**The Danny Angle:** The car. Will's uncle, by the fire: _"A man stands with his own. That's the whole of it."_ — and Danny hears a man in Year One explaining the cars to him through a different kind of smoke. _Pick a car or one picks you._ What Danny chose is not written (the hold is honored — peripheral-shadows register, Stories 1–25). He writes only that he stood where he stood, and that his mother's people have apparently been giving the same answer to that question for seven hundred years.

**The Prison Joke:** In the kirk. Wallace on his knees. Bruce with the sword raised. A hundred men outside gone silent because they can feel the silence inside. Daniel, in the carrying voice of a child who thinks he's whispering: _"Wait. So who has the keys here? You or him?"_ The sword stops. It is the only question in Scotland and a boy from Virginia has asked it in a church. Bruce, finally: _"The one on his knees has the keys, lad. I'm just holding the sword."_ (Consumes `prison_sayings.yaml` "has the keys." **"Embrace the suck"** — originally planned for Wallace — is deliberately preserved for a future Wallace story.)

**The Dad Joke (#1 — the ritual is born):** In the kitchen, in front of Daniel and Jenni, when Jenni asks when he grew the beard: _"I used to hate facial hair... but then it grew on me."_ Daniel: "Dad." Danny: "What." Daniel: "Don't." Jenni: "I'm counting on it." Danny confesses to the journal that a man two cells down told it through a vent in the SHU and he waited five years for a kitchen to tell it in.

**The Fun Police:** The Bureau's Historical Wardrobe Division sends the Prosecutor **Elizabethan** dress — ruff, slashed doublet — three hundred years early; he wears the pinstripe suit underneath for "structural integrity." The Hater gets a monk's habit over his uniform; the hat doesn't fit under the hood; he wears both. Planning to "pre-position for optimal extraction geometry," the Prosecutor climbs into the beaters' pen where the royal hunt's white bull is being held. The Hater opens the gate. The Hater does not close the gate. The Prosecutor ends up on the roof ("tactical"); the Hater is briefly on the bull, then in a tree; the bull is gone into the forest toward the clearing where the Earl of Carrick is about to walk out of a church. **The Fun Police have, for the first time, caused the history they came to prevent.** Nobody will ever know. _"No fun allowed,"_ from the tree, to a squirrel.

**The Lesson:** _You come from people who stepped in front of the thing. That is the whole inheritance — not the name, the step. Names get handed out afterward, by whoever is still standing there when the writing starts. The step is yours. Nobody can give it to you and nobody can take it._ (Lesson #3, rewritten — THEME: LOYALTY / PERMANENCE.)

**Present-Day Canon Introduced:** **Jenni — Jennifer Lewis**, Danny's girlfriend. Forty-seven (DOB July 30, 1978), lead stylist at a very posh high-end salon; natural curly California-surfer "crunchy" blonde hair with a flower in it, bright blue eyes, little or no makeup, bohemian mandala/botanical tattoos on both arms, rings on most fingers, vintage Nirvana tee and leggings (or thrifted chambray), sandalwood; got together with Danny while he was incarcerated and was ultra-loyal for the whole ~5 years; super laid back, new-agey, says "the universe" without irony; a medical marijuana card and a tin of edibles Danny never touches; drives a **purple Jeep Wrangler named Delilah** (parked nose to nose with Jen's green one). Meets Daniel as a man ("You must be the Bull"). Sees the fart. **Is told about the time travel by Daniel, horn in hand, and believes him** — "Okay. Where in Scotland?" She is the only living person who knows, and from here on she is the present-day reader of the Old-Timer's riddles and Daniel's big-sister/best-friend. **It is also moving day:** the Bureau has approved transferring Danny's home detention from Grandpa's house in Palmyra, VA to Jenni's apartment in Northern Virginia just outside D.C., twenty minutes from Daniel; the journal is written at her table. See `character-bible.md`.

**Souvenir:** The black horn-tip of the white bull, pressed into Daniel's hand by Robert Bruce: _"The man who turned it should have a piece of it. The other man will get the land."_

**Continuity Notes:** Danny's beard is period-correct for the first time — note it once, dryly. The Old-Timer appears as a seannachie whittling an oak bull: _"You've been here before. Not you. The shape of you."_ Will o' Rule and the mastiff are available for a Halidon Hill return (1333). Bruce recognizing Daniel at Bannockburn (1314) is an obvious payoff. Wallace is available for Stirling Bridge or Falkirk with "embrace the suck" intact.

### STORY 4 — _THE ARENA_

**Setting:** April 23, 1910 — The Grand Amphitheater of the Sorbonne University, Paris, France. 3:00pm.

**The Real History:** Roosevelt delivered "Citizenship in a Republic" before a crowd including 900 students, ministers in court dress, navy officers in full uniform, and 2,000 ticket holders — with 25,000 people packed in the streets outside. He titled it himself. The world renamed it "The Man in the Arena." He was confused by the French reaction — he admitted privately to Henry Cabot Lodge that he "couldn't quite make out" what the French thought. He was coming off a year-long African safari (funded by _Scribner's Magazine_ in exchange for articles) and was in the middle of a European tour. He was 51, out of office, already restless, already furious with his hand-picked successor Taft for going soft. He would form the Bull Moose Party in 1912.

**The Star:** _Teddy Roosevelt, age 51_ — Older now. Still detonating with energy. But there's something behind the eyes this time — he's been to the jungle, he's seen what happens after the peak of power, and he's chewing on it. He recognizes Danny and Daniel immediately and his enormous face breaks into something almost soft. He calls Daniel "The Bull." He has been calling him that since 1898 in his head. He will never stop.

**Daniel's Role:** Before the speech — in a back corridor of the Sorbonne — Daniel is running, trips, crashes into a wall display of classical paintings, and arrives back to Danny absolutely covered in dust and chalk and something that appears to be red paint but may be blood from a cut on his palm. He's laughing. He looks exactly like "the man in the arena, whose face is marred by dust and sweat and blood." Roosevelt stops walking. Stares. Reaches into his coat for a small notebook.

**The Danny Angle:** Roosevelt pulls Danny aside before he goes on. Asks him: _"Did you give it to him? Did you give that boy a life worth living?"_ Danny says he's trying. Roosevelt says: _"Try harder. Try every single day. That's all the arena asks."_

**The Prison Joke:** During the speech — Danny and Daniel in the audience — TR delivers the famous passage. Daniel leans to Danny and whispers: _"That sounds like what they told you before going on the yard."_ Danny can't speak. He nods once.

**The Dad Joke (#004):** _"What do you call a person who tells dad jokes but has no kids? A faux pa."_

**The Fun Police:** The Prosecutor has obtained a Sorbonne professor's ID and is sitting in the audience with a clipboard, filing a Fun Infraction Citation for "unauthorized emotional inspiration in a public forum." His pen runs out of ink mid-form. He doesn't have another pen. He asks the French professor next to him, who pretends not to speak English.

**The Lesson:** The most important thing isn't being safe from failure. It's being _in it_. Danny: _"Your face was covered in dust and blood and you were laughing. He saw exactly what I see every time I look at you."_

**Continuity Notes:** Roosevelt mentions — casually, as if it's obvious — that he named his new party the Bull Moose Party partly in tribute to a certain eleven-year-old from Brooklyn. Daniel has no idea what he's talking about. This is played completely straight.

### STORY 5 — _THE FLOOD_ (moved from Story 3)

**Setting:** January 15, 1919 — North End, Boston, Massachusetts. The Great Molasses Flood. 2:30pm.

**The Real History:** A 50-foot steel tank holding 2.3 million gallons of molasses exploded, sending a 15-foot wave of molasses through the streets of Boston's North End at 35 miles per hour. 21 people died. 150 were injured. Buildings were flattened. Horses drowned. The molasses was so thick and so cold-weather-viscous that rescue was nearly impossible. The North End at the time was a densely packed Italian immigrant neighborhood. The tank was owned by United Industrial Alcohol — the molasses was being fermented into industrial alcohol. It had been leaking and groaning for weeks and people had been complaining. The company ignored all warnings. The tank literally hummed before it exploded.

**The Star:** _Francesco Losso, fictional Italian immigrant shop owner_ — a real composite of the type. He is seventy-two years old, has 11 grandchildren, survived the Atlantic crossing and still considers it the second worst sea he has seen. He makes the best arancini on the Eastern Seaboard. He is furious about the molasses the way only Italian grandmothers and grandfathers can be furious — comprehensively, architecturally, for generations.

**Daniel's Role:** Daniel hears the tank groaning — he's the first to notice because he's curious and quiet and the world talks to him — and starts pulling people off the street and into a nearby building. He has no idea why. It just sounds wrong. The tank explodes 90 seconds later.

**The Danny Angle:** Danny knows what it looks like when an institution ignores warning signs and people get hurt. He's lived it. The flashback is the moment before something terrible happened in prison that he could feel coming but couldn't stop.

**The Prison Joke:** As they're helping pull a trapped man out of the molasses, Daniel — completely serious, completely calm — says: _"My dad says this is exactly what dead time feels like."_ Danny stares at him. "That's not — that's not what that means." "But it fits though." It fits though.

**The Dad Joke (#005):** _"What's brown and sticky? A stick."_

**The Fun Police:** The Hater, attempting to trap Danny and Daniel, accidentally steps into the molasses and is slowly consumed. He spends the rest of the story slowly, furiously sinking, shouting "NO FUN ALLOWED" at a volume that decreases inversely with his rate of descent.

**The Lesson:** When something is wrong, say something. When you can see it, don't wait for permission to act. Danny: _"The city heard that tank groaning for weeks. Nobody moved. You moved. Remember that."_ (Lesson #5 — moved from #3.)

### STORY 6 — _QUEEN_

**Setting:** 48 BC — Alexandria, Egypt. The Royal Palace. Cleopatra VII is 21 years old, recently deposed by her brother, and is about to meet Julius Caesar for the first time.

**The Real History:** Cleopatra was smuggled into Caesar's quarters rolled inside either a carpet or a linen sack (sources differ), carried in past guards who would have killed her on sight. She was not Egyptian by blood — she was Macedonian Greek, the last of the Ptolemaic dynasty. She was the first of her family in nearly 300 years to bother learning Egyptian. She was fluent in nine languages. She was not considered extraordinarily beautiful by ancient accounts — what contemporaries described was her voice, her intelligence, and her presence. She and Caesar began their affair that same night. Caesar, 52, married to another woman, was absolutely helpless. Later, Marc Antony would be even more so. The woman was genuinely dangerous.

**The Star:** _Cleopatra_ — rendered as a force of intellectual magnetism wrapped in a highly specific political agenda. She is not seductive in the standard Hollywood way — she is _interesting_, which is more lethal. She becomes fixated on Danny almost immediately. Not romantically — she is simply unaccustomed to a man who is not immediately, obviously moved by her. She finds his indifference baffling, then irritating, then a personal challenge. She begins following him around the palace. She sends notes. She has her servants bring him grapes.

**Daniel's Role:** Daniel attempts to explain Cleopatra's interest in Caesar (which they arrived slightly before) to Danny using a combination of historical guessing and instinct. He accidentally helps broker the carpet plan — he's the one who suggests rolling something up when they need to smuggle a message. Cleopatra stares at him, decides he is some kind of oracle, and starts consulting him about everything.

**The Danny Angle:** Danny is intensely uncomfortable being pursued by anyone, for the first time since before prison. _(Continuity note post–Story 3: Danny is now seeing Jenni. Calibrate — his discomfort here is not about availability; it is about being looked at like that by a queen while a woman with a purple Jeep — whose apartment he now lives in, and who knows exactly where he goes on Saturdays — is a few weeks into believing in him. The journal can carry this in one line.)_ Flashback to the life he lost — a marriage, a family — and the man he was before all of it.

**The Prison Joke:** Cleopatra's romantic pursuit of Danny escalates to the point where she is standing outside the room where Danny and Daniel are sleeping, sending messengers at midnight. Daniel sits up, rubs his eyes, looks at the third messenger that night, and says completely flatly: _"She's a Stage 4 clinger."_ Danny inhales sharply. "Where did you hear that?" "Dad, I've watched a lot of TV."

**The Dad Joke (#006):** _"I told my wife she was drawing her eyebrows too high. She looked surprised."_

**The Fun Police:** The Prosecutor attempts to file a "Timeline Contamination" notice on the grounds that Danny and Daniel are interfering with the Caesar-Cleopatra relationship. He is unable to complete the filing because Cleopatra's guards, irritated by his presence in the palace corridor, confiscate his briefcase and use it as a doorstop.

**The Lesson:** People can sense authenticity instantly. The most powerful thing in the room is always the person who doesn't need anything from you. Danny: _"She was used to every man in the world wanting something from her. You know what's rare? A man who doesn't."_

### STORY 7 — _THUNDER_

**Setting:** August 18, 1969 — Woodstock Music and Art Fair, Bethel, New York. 8:45am Monday morning. The crowd has thinned from 500,000 to roughly 40,000. Jimi Hendrix is about to take the stage with his temporary band, Gypsy Sun and Rainbows.

**The Real History:** Hendrix was supposed to close Sunday night. Technical delays pushed him to Monday morning at 9am — when most of the crowd had already left and the remaining 40,000 were exhausted, muddy, and barely awake. The band had barely rehearsed. They "simply could not play well together" according to scholar Joel Brattin. His rhythm guitarist Larry Lee couldn't hear Jimi due to missing monitors and spent most of the set getting mild electric shocks from the wet stage. Hendrix played a chaotic, wandering 2-hour set that was heading toward disaster — then launched into an improvised, unrehearsed version of the Star-Spangled Banner that became one of the most iconic moments in American music history. Martin Scorsese helped edit the Woodstock documentary that made it immortal. The guitar Hendrix played — a white Fender Stratocaster he'd bought the day before the festival — sold for $2 million in 1993. The guitar burning everyone remembers? That was Monterey Pop in 1967, not Woodstock.

**The Star:** _Jimi Hendrix, age 26_ — rendered as someone who exists at a frequency the rest of the world can't quite tune to. He is genuinely soft-spoken offstage. He is in a white jacket with fringe. He carries the guitar like it is an extension of his nervous system. He is profoundly tired. He has been awake for what appears to be several centuries. He is staring at a stage and the remaining crowd and thinking something that no one around him can access. When Danny and Daniel appear backstage, Hendrix looks at Daniel for a long time and then says: _"Hey, little man. You feel that?" Daniel: "Feel what?" Jimi: "Exactly."_

**Daniel's Role:** The band is falling apart mid-set. Hendrix is carrying it alone. Daniel — in the wings, watching — sees a lighter someone dropped, and a roadie's extra guitar leaning against an amp. He picks up the lighter, flicks it idly. Jimi, watching from the stage, sees the flame. Something clicks. The Star-Spangled Banner wasn't in the setlist. It wasn't rehearsed. But Hendrix had watched a small kid flick a lighter backstage and something about the flame against the morning light made him think about burning something beautiful.

**The Danny Angle:** Danny served with men who came back from Vietnam broken. The Star-Spangled Banner as a protest, as a lament, as a complicated love letter to a country that keeps failing its people — Danny knows what that ambivalence feels like. Prison gave him the same relationship with America.

**The Prison Joke:** Backstage, Larry Lee (rhythm guitarist, Vietnam vet, invisible in the official history) is sitting by himself between songs looking at nothing. Daniel sits next to him, and after a while says: _"You just got back from the war, right? My dad says guys like you always got 'back door parole' from that one."_ Lee looks at him a very long time. "...Yeah. Yeah we did, kid."

**The Dad Joke (#007):** _"I used to play piano by ear. Now I use my hands."_ (Jenni, if present in the frame, is the natural audience for a Woodstock-adjacent story — she'd have been there in spirit.)

**The Fun Police:** The Hater, wearing a Woodstock security badge, attempts to confiscate the Entenmann's donuts Danny has been holding since they landed. He is immediately swept up in the crowd of 40,000 mud-covered, sleep-deprived, highly committed festival attendees. He is not seen again until the credits.

**The Lesson:** The beautiful things happen in the gaps — in the moments that weren't planned, weren't scheduled, weren't supposed to happen. Danny: _"He didn't plan to play the anthem. He saw a light and followed it. That's the whole secret, Bull."_

### STORY 8 — _THE UNSINKABLE_

**Setting:** April 14–15, 1912 — The RMS Titanic. 11:40pm, somewhere in the North Atlantic.

**The Real History:** The Titanic had 20 lifeboats — enough for 1,178 people. There were 2,224 people onboard. The officers had received six iceberg warnings that day and ignored them. Captain Edward John Smith had planned this voyage to be his retirement trip. He went down with the ship. The ship's designer Thomas Andrews was aboard and spent the hours after impact helping passengers calmly and professionally before going to the first-class smoking room, where he was last seen staring at a painting of Plymouth Harbor with his arms at his sides. They were paid nothing. They went down with the ship. All eight of them.

### STORY 9 — _THE GREAT STING_

**Setting:** February 14, 1929 — Chicago, Illinois. The Saint Valentine's Day Massacre.

**The Real History:** Seven men — mostly members of Bugs Moran's North Side Gang — were lined up against the wall of a garage at 2122 North Clark Street and shot by four men, two of them dressed as police officers. Al Capone was in Florida at the time, having apparently learned from previous indictments that geographic distance is an excellent alibi. ### STORY 10 — _THE SUMMIT_

**Setting:** May 29, 1953 — 11:30am. The summit of Mount Everest. Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay, 29,028 feet above sea level.

**The Real History:** Hillary and Norgay summited at approximately 11:30am and had roughly 15 minutes at the top before needing to descend. Hillary took photos of Norgay holding flags (ice axe raised) but NOT of himself — there is no photograph of Hillary at the summit, because Norgay, who had never used a camera before, was not asked to take one. The news was released on June 2, 1953 — Queen Elizabeth II's coronation day — by deliberate arrangement. Hillary's first words at base camp were reported to be: _"Well, George, we knocked the bastard off"_ — to expedition leader George Lowe. Norgay's son later said his father cried at the summit. The oxygen equipment they carried was considered experimental. They had 15 minutes of margin before it would run out. At 28,000 feet, Hillary found a crack in his oxygen mask at 1am — meaning he climbed through the night at altitude with compromised oxygen — and fixed it himself in the dark.

**The Star:** _Edmund Hillary_ — rendered as a beekeeper from New Zealand who is somehow, inexplicably, the right man to be the first human being to stand on top of the world. He is large, gentle, slightly embarrassed by the entire thing, and speaks in understatements of such magnitude that they become their own poetry. He once described the summit view as "quite good." He is unreasonably well-adjusted for someone who just did the impossible.

**Daniel's Role:** They land halfway up the mountain — in the Death Zone. Daniel keeps them moving. He also, in the fog and cold and altitude delirium, sees Hillary's oxygen mask beginning to fail and taps him on the shoulder about it. Hillary fixes it. In the dark. In the wind. He later tells people he doesn't know how he spotted it.

**The Danny Angle:** Cold. The kind of cold that makes you bargain with yourself. Danny flashbacks to winter in prison — the SHU in January, a broken radiator, three weeks of it. He kept warm by thinking about Daniel.

**The Prison Joke:** At 28,500 feet, thin air, frostbite threatening, Daniel looks at the terrain above them and then looks at Danny and says, completely casually: _"This is basically doing it the hard way."_ Hillary, overhearing, laughs so hard he almost loses his footing.

**The Dad Joke (#010):** _"Why can't a nose be twelve inches long? Then it would be a foot."_

**The Fun Police:** The Prosecutor has booked a seat on the expedition supply team at Base Camp. He is at 17,000 feet, which is 1,000 feet above his comfort zone. He is filing paperwork in a tent that is flapping at 60 mph. He cannot feel his feet. He still hasn't gotten the form right.

**The Lesson:** The summit is not the point. Getting back down is the point. Most climbing deaths happen on the descent, when people think it's over. Danny: _"The hardest part of anything is after you've already won. Don't let up when you think you're done."_

### STORY 11 — _THE WRIGHT STUFF_

**Setting:** December 17, 1903 — Kill Devil Hills, Kitty Hawk, North Carolina. 10:35am. The first powered, sustained, controlled flight in history.

**The Real History:** The Flyer flew four times that day. The first flight lasted 12 seconds and covered 120 feet. The fourth flight lasted 59 seconds and covered 852 feet. After the fourth flight, a gust of wind flipped the Flyer over and it was destroyed. Only five people were there to witness it — three local lifesaving station crew members, a local businessman, and a young boy named Johnny Moore who happened to be there. There are almost no photographs (Orville took one, of the first flight). The brothers did not immediately go public — it took them several years to file the patent and announce it. They were bicycle mechanics. No one gave them money. No one thought it was possible. They funded the entire project from bicycle shop profits. Wilbur was the serious one; Orville was the gregarious one. Wilbur died nine years later at 45. Orville lived until 1948 — long enough to see jet aircraft, the atomic bomb, and the beginning of the space age.

**The Star:** _Orville Wright, age 32_ — rendered as someone who genuinely cannot believe this is working. He is methodical and enthusiastic in the manner of a man who has spent years building toward something and still, at the actual moment of success, is not quite ready to believe it. He also has absolutely extraordinary mustaches for a man who just invented flight.

**Daniel's Role:** On the third flight, a technical issue with the elevator control causes a problem. Daniel — who has spent hours watching YouTube videos on aerodynamics for a school project — spots it and communicates something that gets corrected before the fourth and longest flight. He uses the wrong words to describe it but the right concept. Orville stares at him and writes something down.

**The Prison Joke:** As Orville prepares for the first flight, the small group watching is dead silent with anticipation. Daniel turns to Danny and whispers: _"Store day energy."_ Danny bites his lip. "That is weirdly accurate." The five witnesses look at them. _(Continuity: "store day" was consumed in Story 2. Select a fresh, unused saying from `prison_sayings.yaml` at outline time.)_

**The Dad Joke (#011):** _"Why did the bicycle fall over? It was two-tired."_

**The Lesson:** Nobody believed them. They did it anyway. With no money, no credentials, and no permission. Danny: _"The world is full of people who will tell you it can't be done. The only answer to that is to do it."_

### STORY 12 — _ROCKET MAN_

**Setting:** July 16–24, 1969 — Kennedy Space Center, Florida, then the Lunar Module, the surface of the Moon. The Apollo 11 mission.

**The Real History:** Neil Armstrong's famous line was supposed to be "one small step for a man" — the "a" was inaudible or missing in transmission, changing the grammar and slightly muddling the meaning. He has said he did say "a man" — it just didn't transmit. Buzz Aldrin described the lunar surface as "magnificent desolation." The computer running the Lunar Module had a processing power roughly equivalent to a modern pocket calculator. When the Eagle landed, it had 17 seconds of fuel remaining. The flag they planted fell over when the engine ignited for liftoff — visible on camera. Michael Collins, orbiting alone above, is one of the most overlooked figures in the story — he spent 21 hours in the Command Module, alone, looping around the far side of the Moon where he was completely unreachable by radio, the most isolated human being in history.

**The Star:** _Buzz Aldrin, age 39_ — rendered as a man who is simultaneously the second most famous person present and absolutely certain he's the most qualified. He has the bearing of someone who has spent considerable time in the presence of very competent people and has decided that being the second person on the Moon is something he will process later. He is currently focused on the checklist. He narrates everything he does in a calm technical voice that somehow makes walking on the Moon sound like parallel parking. He brought communion elements and took communion on the lunar surface — which is real and extraordinary and almost never mentioned.

**Daniel's Role:** Danny and Daniel land not on the Moon but at Mission Control in Houston. Daniel keeps a Mission Controller focused during a tense moment when the computer starts throwing abort codes. He has no idea what he's doing. He just knows, from gaming and computers, that sometimes the right move is to keep going past an error message when the underlying function is fine. The 1202 alarm — which is real — was exactly this situation.

**The Prison Joke:** Deep in the Mission Control moment, Daniel looks at the bank of screens and the rows of NASA engineers and says to Danny: _"This is the most intense stand-up count I've ever seen."_ Three nearby engineers who overhear start laughing despite themselves. The tension breaks. It helped.

**The Dad Joke (#012):** _"Why did the math book look sad? It had too many problems."_

**The Lesson:** Every impossible thing was done by people who decided to keep going past the moment when stopping was reasonable. Danny: _"Those computers kept throwing errors. They flew to the Moon anyway. Past the error, Bull. Always past the error."_

### STORY 13 — _THE LETTER_

**Setting:** April 4, 1968 — Memphis, Tennessee. The Lorraine Motel. The evening of the assassination of Martin Luther King Jr.

**The Real History:** Dr. King was at the Lorraine Motel for the third time to support the Memphis sanitation workers' strike. His room had been changed at the last minute — originally he had a less exposed room; a local official upgraded him to a better room, which turned out to be the exposed one. He had just preached his final sermon — "I've Been to the Mountaintop" — the night before. The sermon contained the now-famous passage about not fearing death and having "been to the mountaintop." Edgar Hoover called him "the most dangerous Negro in America."

**The Star:** _Martin Luther King Jr._ — This story is handled with reverence, not caricature. He is not rendered satirically. He is rendered precisely as he was: a 39-year-old man who is exhausted and afraid and committed absolutely beyond the capacity of fear to change his direction. He is funny in private. He is tender with people he trusts. He carries the weight of an entire movement on a frame that is running on too little sleep and too much faith.

Daniel sits with King in a quiet moment and asks him a question only an eleven-year-old would ask directly: _"Are you scared?"_ King looks at him for a long time and says: _"Every single day."_ Daniel: _"But you keep going anyway."_ King: _"Every single day."_

**The Danny Angle:** This is the emotional center of the early series. Danny writes: _"I have never felt more ashamed of the small things I let break me than I did in that room."_

"He's getting there."

The adventure itself carries no comedy from Danny.

**The Fun Police:** The Hater is present in this story but completely neutered by it. He starts a sentence about "unauthorized inspiration" and cannot finish it. **The Lesson:** Courage is the decision to keep going after fear has made its full argument. Danny: _"He was scared. He told your son. He went to the balcony anyway. That's not fearlessness. That's something better."_

### STORY 14 — _THE ROUGH NECK_

**Setting:** January 9, 1848 — Coloma, California. Sutter's Mill. The moment James Marshall finds gold.

**The Real History:** James Marshall found gold flakes in the water channel he was building for John Sutter's sawmill. He and Sutter tried to keep it secret. They failed immediately. Within months, 300,000 people had flooded into California from around the world — what became the Gold Rush. Sutter's farm was overrun and he never recovered financially. Marshall was no better — he spent the rest of his life making claims on the land where he found gold but never secured rights. He died broke, in 1885. The people who got rich in the Gold Rush were largely not miners — they were people selling supplies, food, and clothing to miners. Levi Strauss made his fortune selling denim pants. **The Star:** _Sam Brannan_ — rendered as the world's first venture capitalist operating on pure audacity. He is fast-talking, expansive, absolutely certain, and the only person in the history of the Gold Rush who saw what was actually happening and positioned accordingly. ### STORY 15 — _THE STORM_

**The Real History:** Katrina struck at Category 4 intensity. ### STORY 16 — _THE ICE MAN_

**Setting:** February 1804 — Near present-day Bismarck, North Dakota. The Lewis and Clark Expedition, wintering at Fort Mandan.

**The Real History:** Lewis and Clark spent the winter of 1804–05 at Fort Mandan, near a Mandan and Hidatsa Native American village. **The Star:** _Meriwether Lewis_ — rendered as an intensely melancholic genius in a coonskin cap who is clearly the most overqualified man for any task he's currently performing and equally clearly suffering from what would today be recognized as severe depression, which he manages through obsessive documentation of absolutely everything. ### STORY 17 — _THE WIRE_

**Setting:** July 22, 1974 — New York City. The Twin Towers of the World Trade Center, under construction. Specifically, between them, 1,350 feet in the air. Philippe Petit is about to walk a wire between the towers.

**The Real History:** Philippe Petit spent eight years planning this. He snuck up the towers the night before, shot a fishing line across the gap with a bow and arrow, and rigged a 450-pound, 7/8-inch steel cable between the buildings. **The Star:** _Philippe Petit, age 24_ — rendered as someone who has organized his entire perception of reality around a single activity and as a result is incapable of being afraid of the thing that terrifies everyone else, while being perfectly normally terrified of everything else. ### STORY 20 — _THE WALL_

**Setting:** November 9, 1989 — Berlin, Germany. The fall of the Berlin Wall. Specifically: the Bornholmer Street checkpoint, 11:15pm.

**The Real History:** The Wall fell because of a mistake. East German Politburo spokesman Günter Schabowski was given a note before a press conference about new travel regulations — regulations that were supposed to be gradual and bureaucratic. He hadn't been briefed. A reporter asked when the new rules took effect. Schabowski shuffled his papers and said: _"Immediately, without delay."_ It was an error. He hadn't read the document. Within hours, thousands of East Berliners were at the checkpoints. The guards — also not briefed — eventually stood aside. People began tearing the Wall apart with hammers. Nobody ordered it. Nobody planned it. A bureaucratic miscommunication ended 28 years of the most heavily fortified border in human history.

**The Star:** _Günter Schabowski, age 60_ — rendered as possibly the most consequential accidental revolutionary in history. **Daniel's Role:** Danny and Daniel are in the crowd at Bornholmer Street. Daniel pushes to the front and is one of the first through — not because he knows it's historical, but because the gate is open and he wants to see what's on the other side. He's always like that. **The Prison Joke:** The moment the gate opens, a guard in a watchtower stares down in bewilderment. Daniel looks up at him and calls: _"Catch out, man! You're back on the bricks!"_ The guard doesn't speak English. But something in the tone makes him smile.

**The Dad Joke (#020):** _"What did one wall say to the other? I'll meet you at the corner."_

**The Lesson:** History doesn't always happen the way it's supposed to. Sometimes the most important things are accidents. Danny: _"A man misread a document. The Wall came down. **The Teddy Thread:** Stories 1, 4 — Roosevelt returns. "Uncle Teddy" is established. His nicknames for Daniel ("The Bull," "my little arena") are seeded.

By Story 95, they come the closest they've ever come. Story 100 is when they almost win. (Story 3 plants the irony that they can *cause* history by failing.)
