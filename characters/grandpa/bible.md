# GRANDPA

## Danny's Father / The House / "Evening."

### Age: Seventies | Present from Story 1

---

Danny lives with his father for Stories 1–2. Grandpa is the house — the kitchen, the driveway, the windowsill plant, the mower that needed a part. He got Danny a modest, Bureau-approved job lead through someone he knows (Story 2). He makes tea. He says "Evening" to whoever is in his kitchen — a boy with goggles, a woman with rings, a bull horn on the table — and asks no questions, which is not incuriosity but a form of manners so old it reads as mystery. He claims to have told Danny every dad joke first. He has not.

**The Turnbull line runs through him.** His mother was **Helen Trumbull** — the Turnbulls of Rule Water, the name americanized at the port generations back. She told Danny the bull story; Grandpa never has. He is the quiet, living end of a seven-hundred-year line that begins with a man stepping in front of a bull, and the series lets that sit under "Evening" without ever pointing at it. (Both of Danny's parents are living. **Grandma** — Danny's mother — exists and is unstoried; whether she lives in the house is not established. All four of Danny's grandparents are deceased.)

**Story 3:** Danny moves out, to Jenni's apartment, with the Bureau's blessing. Grandpa helps load the Buick. He says "Evening" to the empty kitchen. From Story 4 on he is a place they visit, not the frame.

**What is NOT established:** his name, his history, what he thinks about any of it, how he and Danny are with each other when Daniel isn't there. These are held. He is written as peripheral, specific, and warm, and he is never used for exposition.

**Rules:** Appears in present-day frames only. Never more than a few lines. Never surprised by anything. Makes tea.
