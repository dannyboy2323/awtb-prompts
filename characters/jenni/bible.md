## 1. THE CORE TRUTH

Jenni is the woman who was there before the door opened.

She got together with Danny while he was still inside — not after, not as a reward for surviving, but *during*, when he had nothing to offer and a number instead of a name. She wrote. She drove to the fence. She put money on the books. She sat in the visiting room across a bolted-down table, in a room built to make everyone small, and was not small. For five years she was loyal in the plain, unglamorous, un-poetic way that is the only way loyalty is ever tested: every week, on time, in the same purple Jeep, saying the same four words at the end of every visit — *See you Saturday, Danny.*

She is not a replacement for anyone. She is not a prize. She is what it looks like when a man who has decided to be present finds someone who decided to be present first — in her own completely different idiom. A woman who says "the universe" without irony and can back a trailer. Who wears a flower in her hair to a federal prison visiting room and gets a smile out of a corrections officer. Who grins behind both hands at a fart that drops the kitchen fifteen degrees, and who, handed a bull's horn from 1297 and an eleven-year-old's flat explanation of where it came from, says "Okay. Where?" — and means it.

She is warm. She is funny without trying. She is a little bit magic in the specifically un-magical way of people who assume the world is stranger and kinder than it looks. The series likes her. Daniel, after exactly one second of assessment, likes her. That is the whole audition and she passes it.

**The structural point:** the series is a love letter from a father to a son. Jenni is the proof that the father was loved back while he was at his lowest — and that the person who loved him is now the one who opens her door to him (and his whole chaotic life) on the first day he's allowed to walk through one.

## 3. WHO SHE IS

- **Jennifer Lewis.** Goes by **Jenni, with an i.** Born July 30, 1978 — a year ahead of Danny (b. July 3, 1979), which she mentions.
- **Lead stylist at a very exclusive, very high-end salon in the Washington, D.C. area.** The kind of place with a waitlist, a doorman or a discreet buzzer, and a clientele that includes people from magazines, the Hill, and television. She has had her hands in the hair of people you have heard of and will never name them (client confidentiality is *the* thing she is old-fashioned about). She is the best in the building and does not talk about it much. It is why she asks about the beard with professional interest — and why the answer to her is the first dad joke of the series.
- **The vibe:** bohemian / hippy / gypsy-soul. California-blond surfer meets crunchy new-age free spirit. *Light* — she walks into a room and it gets a degree warmer. Super laid back. New-age without being soft: she believes in the universe, intention, moon phases, energy, the way a stone feels in your hand, and also in showing up on time, cash tips, and a sharp pair of shears. The two halves do not fight. She reads people the way she reads hair: texture, history, what it wants to do if you let it.
- **The card.** She has a legal medical marijuana card and enjoys her edibles — a tin of gummies on a shelf, a habit she keeps without apology and without pushing. **Danny does not partake** — he is on federal supervision and the series never lets him near it. The story notes this exactly once, dryly, and moves on. (Writers: never play her high for comedy; never let the card become a plot. The laugh at the dad jokes is not the gummies.)
- **Not married, never married. No kids of her own.** She chose the shears and the road; she is not wistful about it.
- **The purple Jeep.** A **purple Jeep Wrangler named Delilah** — the color of a bruise healing — her pride and joy, always hers, never swapped (unlike Jen's rotating cars). She talks to Delilah; Daniel, who talks to inanimate objects, recognizes a colleague. Delilah is the first thing Daniel notices about her and the first thing he says. Delilah carried her to the prison fence every week for five years — the Jeep is a witness. *(Spelling "Delilah" is the v1.2 canon and is used here; `[AUTHOR TO CONFIRM]` the author hasn't restated the name.)*
- **Her apartment.** In **Northern Virginia, just outside D.C.**, about **twenty minutes from Daniel** (and from the salon). An apartment that has been prepared, quietly, for five years: a plant-heavy, thrift-and-vintage, candle-and-sandalwood, record-player-and-crystals-on-the-sill home with a kitchen table that actually seats three. Barefoot-only floor. She has kept a closet and a drawer empty for a year and has never mentioned it. (See §6.)

## 4. HOW SHE AND DANNY GOT TOGETHER — THE LOYALTY STORY

*(Author direction, Nov 18, 2026: "she got together with Dad Danny while he was incarcerated and was ultra loyal and supportive and loving throughout the ordeal.")*

**What is canon (the spine):**
- Danny and Jenni became a couple **while he was in federal prison.** Not before the sentence; not after release.
- She was **ultra-loyal, supportive and loving** throughout the whole sentence — roughly **five years** (approximately 97 months is the number in the timeline; Story 3 says "five years").
- She never made him earn it, never asked for the story of what he did, and never once made him feel like a case.

**The texture (reconciliation — written so it can be changed in one place `[AUTHOR TO CONFIRM]`):**
- She came into his life through the mail and the visiting room, not the hardware store. (The v1.2 "met at the hardware store three weeks after release" is **retired** — it contradicted the author's direction.)
- **The first visit.** Danny told her flat, at the start, exactly what he was and what he'd done — the way he tells things. She said: *"Okay."* Then: *"What are you reading in here?"* That was the moment, for him. (v1.2 had this as "the second date, after release"; it now happens at the first visit. The line is unchanged — it is the line.)
- **The routine.** Saturdays. She drove the purple Jeep to the fence, signed in, took off the rings that set off the detector, sat in a plastic chair in a room built to make everyone small, and was not small. She sent books he'd mentioned, one at a time. She put money on his books. She wrote letters on the back of salon appointment cards and receipts and once, memorably, on a paper placemat. She kept every letter back.
- **Her last words every visit:** *"See you Saturday, Danny."* Never "goodbye." Never "stay strong." Never once a promise. A schedule. (Danny quotes it in a flashback in a later story; it is available to every writer.)
- **What she did not do:** she did not leave when it got long, did not ask him for a promise about who he would be, did not "wait" as a performance. She *lived her life* — worked, cut hair, drove Delilah to festivals — and kept the Saturday. That is the loyalty: not suspended animation, an appointment.
- **What it cost her:** Never stated; never needs to be. The reader sees it in the way she looks at the kitchen table on moving day.
- **Why it works for the series:** it makes the present-day frame (her apartment) feel *earned* rather than convenient, and it echoes the series' central structure — someone showing up, week after week, because that is what love is.

**Relationship to the Jen canon.** In v1.2, Jen was "his one real relationship" and Jenni was his first after prison. Now Jenni's relationship *overlaps the end of his marriage's long tail and the sentence itself.* **Rule:** the series never dates the exact month Danny and Jenni became a couple, never lines it up against the divorce, and never lets any character comment on the overlap. (`[AUTHOR TO CONFIRM]` the order of events; if it matters later, the author picks the date.)

## 5. STORY 3 — THE BIG MOVE (the frame she enters)

*(Author direction, Nov 18, 2026.)* Story 3 is **moving day.** After 5 years in federal prison and the first months of home confinement at **Grandpa's house in Palmyra, Virginia**, Danny has finally received **permission from the Federal Bureau of Prisons / his probation-supervision officer to transfer his home confinement to Jenni's apartment in Northern Virginia, just outside D.C.** — **twenty minutes from Daniel.** Palmyra is a long, long way from Daniel; this move closes that distance for the first time since the day he went in.

**Why this is a big deal (the beats Danny never states):**
- *Five years of a bunk, a locker, a count, a door that locks from the outside — and now a door he has a key to.*
- *He is moving **in** with a woman who has waited for him, into a home she prepared.* He is not a visitor in her life; he is a resident. He will wake up in a room with a window and a person who made coffee.
- *Twenty minutes.* Danny writes the number, not the word "closer." It is the number he had been counting since the sentencing.
- *Grandpa stays behind in the house in Palmyra.* The last Saturday in the Palmyra kitchen is both an ending and a release; Grandpa's "Evening" goes to an empty kitchen that night and the story does not comment.
- The windowsill plant goes with him. It is the same plant that was in the window in Stories 1 and 2.

**How Jenni is introduced (the first impression readers get):**
- **She is in the kitchen when Daniel arrives** because Delilah is in the driveway with the back seats folded and half of Danny's life in it. She was going to take the first load over before Daniel came. Danny asked her to stay. One exchange, no interiority — *the bravest thing he does in the story that doesn't involve a bull.*
- **Delilah and the green Jeep nose to nose in the driveway** for forty seconds; Jen doesn't get out; the two-fingers-off-the-wheel salute; nobody comments.
- **"You must be the Bull."** She doesn't stand. She doesn't do the big voice. Daniel: "Your Jeep's purple." She: "It is." Daniel: "My mom's is green." She: "I know. I saw her pull out. Nice Jeep." Negotiation complete.
- **Dad Joke #1 (the facial-hair joke)** — she asks professionally when he grew the beard; Danny: *"I used to hate facial hair… but then it grew on me."* She laughs the real laugh. Daniel: "He's going to do this every time now. You know that, right?" Jenni: "I'm counting on it."
- **She sees the fart (the frost on the window) and not the crack.** She is told afterward; she believes.

*Why Danny only needs a few sentences to introduce her:* the reader who has been paying attention since Story 1 already felt the hair tie on the counter in Story 3's opening; the introduction can be small because the *relationship* is five years deep.

## 6. THE APARTMENT

- **Location:** Northern Virginia, just outside D.C. — the exact town is **never named in the series** (`[AUTHOR TO CONFIRM]` if you'd like a real place). Twenty minutes from Daniel in normal traffic, which Danny measures in his head every time he drives it.
- **Interior (kitchen, bath, bedroom, living room):** thrifted furniture and vintage band posters (Nirvana, Joni Mitchell, Fleetwood Mac), a record player, plants everywhere (a pothos at least two decades old), crystals on windowsills, a rolled yoga mat, a tin of gummies on a high shelf, a kitchen table that seats three because she bought it to.
- **Prepared for him:** one closet half empty for a year; a drawer cleared; a windowsill left bare for **the plant**; a spare bed made up in the "Daniel room" that is *not* called a guest room.
- **Rule:** the kitchen table is the new present-day frame for the journal ending (replacing Grandpa's table), though Grandpa's house remains a place they visit.
- **Detail for Danny's POV:** the first thing he checks, every night, is whether the door is unlocked from the *inside*. It is. He checks again.

## 7. WHAT SHE KNOWS

- **Knows:** that Danny did time and why (he told her at the first visit); that Daniel is the center of his life; that Jen exists, is a good mother, and drives a different Jeep/car each story; and — from the end of Story 3 — **the time travel.** She is the only living person who knows.
- **Saw (Story 3):** the fart — the pressure change, the frost on the window, the cold, the towel lifting, the boy looking at *her* to see if she saw it. She did. She grinned behind both hands.
- **Did not see:** the crack. To her, nothing happened between the fart and the two of them standing there blinking. No time passed. (The world rule holds: bystanders never see the crack.)
- **Was told (Story 3):** Daniel, horn-tip in hand, before Danny could decide how to handle it: *"We went to Scotland. Seven hundred years ago. There was a bull."* Jenni: *"Okay. Where in Scotland?"* That was the whole conversation. She did not need convincing and she did not perform believing. She believed him the way she believes in moon phases — as a fact about the world she simply hadn't been told yet.
- **Does not know (yet):** what the Sacred Eternal Fire is. Who the Old-Timer is. The Fun Police. Those are held from everyone.

## 8. HER ROLE IN THE SERIES

- **The interpreter of the strange.** Danny's instinct is to hold the mystery; Daniel's is to experiment on it (the DONUT EXPERIMENTS notebook); Jenni's is to *read* it. Her new-age frame — time as a circle, energy that remembers, standing in two places at once — is the first present-day vocabulary for what the Old-Timer keeps saying. When Daniel brings home a nugget, she is the one who sits with him at the table and takes it apart. She is sometimes right; the series never confirms which times.
- **The shore that is not a replacement for the shore.** Jen is the shore the boy returns to; Jenni is the **door that stays open** — the home Danny returns to.
- **The audience.** The Sacred Eternal Fire likes an audience. She is the first one — and the first witness who was told the truth and kept it.
- **The proof of Danny's worth.** Not as a trope — as a fact the narration never states: someone chose him at his lowest.
- **She does not travel.** (If she ever does, it is the single most consequential structural decision in the series after the journal; the author's call.)

## 9. JENNI AND DANIEL

She did not do the big voice. She did not stand up. She held out her hand and said, "You must be the Bull," and he shook it and looked at her for the one second he gives everyone, and then negotiated the Jeeps, and it was over. She does not try to parent him — ever. What grows instead is **brother and sister, best friends.** She is forty-seven with a youthful spirit and a young energy, and Daniel, who is eleven with an old soul, meets her exactly in the middle. They have jokes Danny isn't in on. They have a running argument about Delilah versus the Buick. She is the one he texts the notebook results to. She is the only adult who will sit on the kitchen floor at eleven at night and say "okay, but what if time isn't a line" and mean it as a question for him to answer. He calls her *Jenni-with-an-i*. He has decided she is fine, which from Daniel is a lifetime appointment. She is also, quietly, the first person outside the car he ever told the truth about where they go.

**What Daniel knows (and the series honors):** she was there *before*. He knows she visited his dad. He has heard her say "See you Saturday, Danny" in his head, secondhand, as a thing his dad does in his sleep. He never mentions it to her; it is understood.

## 10. JENNI AND JEN

They said "Hi" across a hood. Both meant it. That is the entire relationship on the page and it must stay that way: two women who have each decided to be good at this, with no scene in which either is diminished, competed with, or used to make a point about the other. Jen is *not* a villain, not bitter, and knows Jenni was in Danny's life during the sentence (`[AUTHOR TO CONFIRM]` — the series never examines how she feels about it; her two-fingers-off-the-wheel salute covers it). If the series ever puts them in a room together, it is to show two adults being adults.

## 11. THE DAD JOKES

She laughs. The real laugh, the one surprised out of her. Every time. Danny cannot explain this and never tries. Daniel finds it concerning. "He's going to do this every time now. You know that, right?" "I'm counting on it."

## 12. VOICE

- **Speech:** easy, warm, unhurried, short sentences; a little California in the vowels, a little vintage-slang ("dude" is Jenni's, *not* the Prosecutor's — it comes out of her as a compliment). She asks questions rather than making statements. She says "the universe" and "energy" sincerely and "Okay" like a door opening.
- **Her signature "Okay."** Not agreement — acceptance. It is the same "Okay" she gave Danny's sentence and Daniel's Scotland. When she says it, the scene breathes out.
- **What she doesn't say:** she doesn't say "I waited." She doesn't say "I knew." She says "See you Saturday."
- **Humor:** dry underneath the sunshine. Teasing, never mean. She is the only person who teases Danny about the beard (professionally) and gets away with it.
- **With Delilah:** talks to it ("C'mon, girl.") — Daniel approves.

---

## 13. MYTHIC LAYER

If Jen is the forge and Danny the flame and Daniel the keeper, Jenni is **the weather** — the thing that arrives from outside the house, uninvited and unforecast, and turns out to be exactly what the ground needed. It is not an accident that the Story 3 fart is the weather register, or that the frost on the window takes the shape of a hillside she will never see. She is also **the Saturday** — the appointment that held through five years — and the series' quiet answer to "what does a person do when they love someone they can't reach": *show up on schedule.*

## 14. BEHAVIORAL RULES FOR WRITERS

1. Jenni is never a rival to Jen and is never written in comparison to her. Not by Danny, Daniel, or the narration.
2. She is warm, funny, present, and a little bit strange. She is never ditzy. The new-age vocabulary is sincere and she is competent in the practical world (she can back a trailer, change a tire, fix a kitchen faucet).
3. She laughs at every dad joke. Fixed trait.
4. She sees the fart. She does not see the crack. She **knows about the time travel from the end of Story 3** and is the only living person who does. She never tells Jen. She never tells Grandpa. The secret is hers and theirs.
5. She asks the next question — the right one, the practical one ("Where in Scotland?" "Did he eat?" "Was the old man there again?"). She is never a skeptic and never a cheerleader; she is a reader of the strange. Her new-age interpretations of the Old-Timer's nuggets are offered as questions and never confirmed by the narration.
6. Danny writes about her in **three sentences at a time, at most**, and never explains her. (Exception, once, with weight: the visiting room / "See you Saturday" flashback. Even there, no more than five sentences.)
7. She drives a **purple Jeep Wrangler named Delilah**. The purple Jeep and Jen's rotating car may share a driveway. Nobody comments.
8. She treats Daniel as a person, not a project. Their bond is sibling / best-friend — jokes, texts, floor-sitting, the notebook. She never mothers him; the series never lets her try.
9. She is a supporting character in the present-day frame. She does not travel.
10. Her edibles are a character detail, not a bit. Danny never partakes. Nobody is ever written high.
11. From Story 3, the present-day frame is her apartment. Grandpa's house is where they visit.
12. **Her loyalty is never converted into debt.** Danny's love for her is not gratitude; the narration never frames her as someone he "owes." (She would hate that.)
13. **She is not saved by Danny and not saving him.** She is a full adult with her own career, friends, money, and life; Danny arrives into it.
14. **The salon is hers.** She is the lead stylist at a very exclusive DC-area salon; she is respected there; the series never makes her subordinate to a boss or a man in that world. Clients' names are never given.
15. She never speaks about the specific crimes or the details of Danny's sentence to anyone. It is his to tell.
16. In flashbacks of the visiting room, she is the **only** person in the room not diminished by it; write her as the largest thing in the frame.
