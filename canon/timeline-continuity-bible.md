## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

This document has two functions:

**Function 1 — Reference before writing.** Before generating any story, consult this document for: Danny and Daniel's current ages, Danny's supervision status (home detention → supervised release), what the Old-Timer has said previously, what souvenirs Daniel has collected, which assets have been consumed, which dad jokes and fart registers are spent. Use it to ensure the story you're about to write is consistent with everything that came before.

**Function 2 — Update after writing.** Every completed story must be logged here: the souvenir Daniel took, the prison joke deployed, the dad joke told, the fart register used, the asset items consumed, the images generated, any new fact established about the characters or world. The document grows with the series. A continuity error discovered in Story 400 can be traced back to an unlogged fact in Story 23.

## THE MASTER CHRONOLOGY

### COMPLETE CHRONOLOGICAL HISTORY — ALL CHARACTERS

---

**~1297 (the deep past — established Story 3)**

- In the Ettrick Forest, Scotland, a Borders family from Rule Water — the Rules of Bedrule — stand with Wallace's host in the weeks after Stirling Bridge. A young giant, William of Rule, finishes a white bull that an eleven-year-old boy from the future has turned away from Robert Bruce, Earl of Carrick. Bruce dubs him "Turn-e-bull." The name becomes a clan.
- _This is Danny's **father's** line: Grandpa's mother was **Helen Trumbull**, the name americanized from Turnbull when her great-grandparents immigrated. The series treats it as literal ancestry. History (Boece, the 1315 Philiphaugh charter) "rounded it off."_

**1979**

- **July 3:** Danny Boice is born. The Fourth of July kid who arrived a day early.
- _Continuity note: His mother referenced this timing as "explaining everything about him." This detail, if used in a flashback, is always framed as his mother's observation, not Danny's._

**~1990 (Danny age 11, same age as Daniel at series start)**

- Danny's early computer abilities emerge. Self-taught. The school system has no framework for what he's doing.
- Danny's grandmother, **Helen Trumbull** (his father's mother), tells him the Turnbull bull story in her basement — a man named Rule, a king, a charging bull, a name. He files it under things grandmothers say. (Established Story 3. She, and all four of Danny's grandparents, are deceased by series start. Both of his parents are living.)
- _This is a deliberate mirror: Danny at 11 was already extraordinary in ways the world couldn't categorize. Daniel at 11 is the same. This parallel can be drawn explicitly in a later-series flashback._

**~1997–2001 (Danny, late teens/early 20s)**

- Danny builds his technical skills independently, takes on contract work, begins accumulating real expertise.
- _No college degree is explicitly established. This is intentional — left open. He is self-taught and self-made._

**~2001–2008 (Danny, early career)**

- Danny rises through the software industry. Genuinely brilliant. Reaches executive level.
- _The specific companies he worked for are never named in early stories. By mid-series, fragments emerge. By late series, the full picture is available. (`adventures.md` Story 2 supplies an author-written "Trustify" flashback — see the Story 2 outline's Continuity Flag 1; the company name, charges, and sentence length there are NOT yet reconciled with the locked facts below.)_

**~2012–2013 (Danny ~33–34)**

- Danny meets Jen. Both in their early-to-mid 30s.
- _How they met: not yet locked in. Writers should not invent this detail without authorization — hold it for a designated story._

**~2013–2014 (Danny ~34–35, Jen ~early 30s)**

- Danny and Jen's relationship deepens. They are poorly matched in lifestyle — she wants stability, he is constitutionally incapable of it — and perfectly matched in something harder to name.
- _She was his one real relationship. The only person who ever got fully inside the armor._

**~2014 (Danny ~35, Jen ~mid-30s)**

- Daniel is born. Danny names him Daniel immediately.
- Calls him The Bull from day one — the boy arrived in the world with the specific energy of something that intended to be there.
- _Daniel's exact birth date is not locked in. His age at series start (11, summer 2026) establishes that Daniel was born approximately 2014–2015. Writers: DO NOT assign a specific birth date without authorization. Use "around 2014" or "when Daniel was born."_

**2014–2019 (Daniel ages 0–5/6)**

- The family years. Danny puts Daniel to bed every night, arm in arm. They go on small adventures — hikes, trips, the kind of outings that became sacred to both of them before they were old enough to understand why.
- Danny's career continues to accelerate. The marriage is under increasing strain — not from conflict, but from Danny's intensity, his pace, his tendency to always be at the edge of something.
- _These years are the foundation the series points back to. They were real and good. The divorce and prison erased access to them but not the fact of them._

**~2019 (Danny ~40, Jen ~late 30s, Daniel ~5)**

- Danny and Jen divorce.
- _Timing note: The divorce occurred before the federal case. This is important — Danny did not drag Jen through the criminal proceedings as his spouse. She was already out. This is Danny's one mercy to her, though he did not frame it that way at the time._
- _The divorce was mutual in the legal sense and clearly necessary in the practical sense. Jen had reached her limit. Danny knew it and couldn't change the underlying reality. It was not dramatic. It was the end of something that had been ending for a while._
- Jen retains primary custody of Daniel, who is approximately 5 at the time.
- _Continuity note: Daniel's last clear memory of his father before prison is from this period — around age 5–6. He does not fully remember the divorce. He remembers the before and then the after. Prison is what changed the before into an absence._

**~2019–2020 (Danny ~40–41)**

- Danny is founder and CEO of **Trustify**, an internet startup (author's Story 2 brief, `adventures.md`). The company goes wrong. Danny takes the fall for a great deal of malfeasance — some his own, some belonging to others.
- The federal case: **securities fraud and money laundering.** _(This supersedes the earlier "unauthorized computer access + conspiracy" draft wording in older documents.)_
- _Continuity note: What exactly Danny did and didn't do, who else was involved, and why he took the fall are DELIBERATELY UNRESOLVED in early stories. The full picture emerges across flashbacks in Stories 50–200. Story 2 established the COO/recording/mercy flashback as the "warning shot he missed." Writers: do not invent specifics of the crime beyond Trustify, the charges, and the fact that he did not cooperate._

**Early 2020 (Danny 40)**

- Federal arrest. The case moves quickly.
- _Danny does not cooperate. He does not give up anyone. He accepts the charges and takes the time. This is established early and repeatedly._
- Federal sentence: **97 months.** He will serve roughly six and a half years inside — early 2020 to June 2026 — before First Step Act and good-time credits move him to home confinement.
- _Assigned facility: one of the most violent federal prisons in the United States — a designation resulting from Danny's security classification, which was elevated by an incident in his first weeks inside._
- Danny enters federal prison. Daniel is approximately **5–6 years old.**
- _This is the wound. Five, six years old. The last real memory Daniel has of his father before the visits began._

## DANNY'S PRISON TIMELINE — YEAR BY YEAR

This framework governs all prison flashbacks across the series. Every flashback must be consistent with this chronology. Details not covered here can be invented by writers within the spirit of what is established, and must then be logged here.

---

**YEAR 1 — 2020 (Danny age 41)**

**The Adjustment.**

Danny enters a violent federal institution with a white-collar crime conviction and a security classification that marks him as someone the system has decided needs watching. This is its own kind of danger.

_Established facts:_

- The incident that elevated his security classification happens in the first weeks. What it was: NOT YET REVEALED. Held for a significant flashback in Stories 75–100.
- **The cars (Story 3, pending manuscript):** In the first week a man — no name, a shape, a voice — explained the cars to Danny the way you explain weather. _"You're going to get asked. Everybody gets asked. Pick a car or one picks you."_ Danny learned there is no such animal as a lone wolf inside. He stood where he stood. **With whom: HELD.** This flashback reveals the structure, not the choice.
- He survives Year 1. He will not elaborate, even in the journal.
- The adjustment is real and hard and Danny's silence about it in the journal is itself the information.

_Prison flashback guidance for Year 1:_

- Physical adjustment — the violence, the noise, the specific fear of not knowing the rules
- The first visit from Daniel (age 6, approximately) — the most emotionally significant event of Year 1, mentioned in passing across multiple early stories before the full scene is rendered
- The beginning of learning to be still

---

**YEAR 2 — 2021 (Danny age 42)**

**The SHU.**

Danny spends time in solitary confinement (the SHU — Special Housing Unit). Duration and specific reason for the SHU placement are NOT YET REVEALED. Held for a mid-series flashback.

_Established facts:_

- The SHU is where he reads the most. Where he thinks the most. Where he has the most time.
- The SHU is where the Hemingway begins — he reads everything in the library before and during SHU, but the SHU is where he memorizes the parts that matter.
- The SHU is where he understands what he did and begins deciding who he wants to be.
- The silence during SHU stints is the deepest pain — no visits, no calls, no letters in or out.
- **The first dad joke (Story 3):** a man two cells down told it through a vent — _"I used to hate facial hair... but then it grew on me."_ Danny waited five years for a kitchen to tell it in.

_Prison flashback guidance for Year 2:_

- Isolation. Not presented as melodrama. Presented as what it is: time, silence, the specific terror of being completely alone with yourself.
- The journal begins in the SHU — or at least the impulse to document does. The actual physical journal may not exist yet (no materials), but the mental practice of writing it starts here.
- Danny does not break. He bends, in the McCarthy sense — the tree in the wind. This is a distinction the series makes carefully.

---

**YEAR 3 — 2022 (Danny age 43)**

**The Library.**

Danny becomes the de facto manager of the prison library. He reads everything. He teaches other inmates to read. He helps men prepare for GED exams.

_Established facts:_

- This continues for approximately two years (Years 3–4).
- This is where the serious self-education happens — not just Hemingway but history, philosophy, science, law.
- This is where Danny meets the friend who does not survive. (Not yet named. Not yet described in detail. Held for a story in the 50–150 range.)
- This is also where he meets the friend who is released before him and whom Danny hasn't found yet at series start.
- **Visitation (Stories 2–3):** the visiting room is where Daniel absorbed the slang — "store day," "money on my books," "has the keys" — and the "Big Poppa" honorific the older men used for respected fathers. "Has the keys" is specifically dated to Year 3 visitation in the Story 3 outline.

_Prison flashback guidance for Year 3:_

- The library scenes should be rendered with specificity — particular books, particular inmates, the specific texture of a space that is quieter than the rest of the facility.
- Danny teaching reading to someone is a recurring, gentle detail in the flashbacks — never dwelt on, always present.
- The two friendships develop here. They are real. They are not sentimental.

---

**YEAR 4 — 2023 (Danny age 43–44)**

**The Worst Year.**

The details of Year 4 are held. Something happens that is the lowest point of Danny's incarceration — not the violence (that was Year 1), not the isolation (Year 2), but something that is about choice and its cost. This is the year that the prison flashbacks are building toward across the first 100 stories.

_Established facts:_

- He does something in Year 4 that he carries differently than the other things he carries. Not shame exactly — something more specific and harder to name.
- He did hard things for real reasons. He survived what should have broken him.
- He comes out of Year 4 different — not broken, but reconfigured.

_Prison flashback guidance for Year 4:_

- Do not reveal Year 4's central event until it is designated for revelation (approximately Stories 100–150).
- Approach it. Describe its edges. Let Danny get close and pull back. This restraint is the technique — the reader knows something is there before they know what it is.

---

**YEARS 5–6 — 2024 to June 2026 (Danny age 44–46)**

**The Countdown.**

Danny knows he is getting out — the credits are adding up, the date keeps moving closer. The last stretch of a federal sentence is its own psychological terrain — hope mixed with fear, the forward-leaning that can be as destabilizing as the despair.

_Established facts:_

- He is still writing — now in actual notebooks, which he acquires through the library system.
- Daniel is approximately 10, then 11, during these years. The visits have continued. The boy keeps coming. Danny writes about this in the journal — the specific quality of Daniel at 10, the way he has grown without Danny seeing most of it.
- The friend who doesn't survive — this loss occurs before the end of Year 5. The specific timing within this year is held for designation to a specific story.
- The preparation begins: who Danny wants to be when he gets out, what he owes, what he can still do.

_Prison flashback guidance for Year 5:_

- The anticipation of release is complicated. The fear that the world has moved on. The fear that he won't know how to be a father to an eleven-year-old he has seen only in visits.
- The preparation for the journal — the decision, made consciously sometime in Year 5, that he is going to write it all down for Daniel.

---

**RELEASE — June 2026 (Danny age 46)**

Danny is released from the facility to **federal home detention** (ankle monitor) in June 2026. Home detention runs to **August 2027**. Then **three years of supervised release** — August 2027 to August 2030 — with early termination possible for good conduct. There is **no restriction on his computer use.** He never calls any of it "parole."

_First actions:_

- Getting to Daniel. This is the organizing principle of his first days out.
- He moves into his father's house (Grandpa — present from Story 1).
- The first real adventure together — Story 1 — happens within the first weeks of his release (a Saturday in mid-June 2026). Danny is raw. Newly out. The world is strange and Daniel is eleven years old and standing right there.
- Story 2 (the second Saturday): the ankle monitor; the officer behind the desk; a modest Bureau-approved job lead through his father.
- Story 3 (the third Saturday, late June 2026): Jenni has been in Danny's life since the sentence (together while he was inside; loyal for the whole ~5 years). **It is moving day** — the Bureau has approved transferring his home detention from Grandpa's house in Palmyra, VA to Jenni's apartment in Northern Virginia just outside D.C., twenty minutes from Daniel. The Buick and Delilah are loaded nose to nose in Grandpa's driveway when the donuts come out. By the end of the story Jenni knows about the time travel.
- Danny turns 47 on July 3, 2026 — between Story 3 and Story 4. (A birthday beat is available to Story 4 or 5; not required.)

_Continuity note:_ Danny entered prison in early 2020 at age 40–41. He comes home in June 2026 at 46. Story 1 begins within weeks of his release — he is 46, Daniel is 11, and the adventures begin almost immediately. This is intentional and emotionally correct: the series begins when Danny is at his most raw, most uncertain, most hungry to make things right. He has not had years to settle back into civilian life. He is figuring it out in real time, alongside Daniel, adventure by adventure.

**STORY DENSITY AND TIME PASSAGE**

Each adventure (a single story) takes approximately one day in real-world time — the afternoon and evening Danny and Daniel spend together, plus the adventure itself (which takes no real-world time regardless of its duration), plus Danny writing in the journal that night.

Danny and Daniel typically see each other on weekends and school holidays. Stories 1–3 are consecutive Saturdays (Story 2: "Second Saturday"; Story 3: "Third Saturday"). This governs how frequently adventures can plausibly occur:

- During school year: roughly 1–2 adventures per week (weekends)
- During school breaks: more frequent
- Summers: potentially multiple per week

## THE PRISON FLASHBACK FRAMEWORK

### Governing Rules for All Flashbacks

---

### THE ESCALATION SCHEDULE

Prison flashbacks deepen and reveal more across the series in a planned sequence. Writers must honor where in the series a given story falls and calibrate the flashback depth accordingly.

**Stories 1–25 — Peripheral Shadows:**
Flashbacks establish the fact of prison without specifics. What it was like in general. The noise. The counting. The silence of the SHU. The way time moves differently. Danny's distance from Daniel. These flashbacks are atmospheric, not narrative.

_What they reveal:_ That prison was real and hard and that the hardest part was Daniel.
_What they do NOT reveal:_ Anything about the specific incident that elevated his security classification; anything about what he did in Year 4; the friend who didn't survive.

_Logged so far:_ Story 1 — the SHU, push-ups, counting breaths, a hill with Daniel. Story 2 — pre-prison: the COO, the recording, the mercy, the two-sided desk. Story 3 (pending) — Year 1, the cars; the choice withheld.

**Stories 26–75 — The Friends:**
The two friends in prison begin to emerge as real people. The one who ran the library with Danny. The one who is released before Danny. The one who doesn't survive. We start to understand that Danny built real relationships inside, and that those relationships carried real loss.

_What they reveal:_ That Danny was not isolated or hardened into a loner. That he found and maintained loyalty inside.
_What they do NOT reveal:_ The specifics of either friend (names are held; what happened to the one who doesn't survive is held).

**Stories 76–125 — The Crime:**
The story of what Danny actually did — the discovery of fraud, the decision to extract evidence, the consequences — begins to emerge in fragments. By Story 125, the reader has the full broad-strokes picture.

_What they reveal:_ The motivation and broad outlines of the crime.
_What they do NOT reveal:_ Full details of what he did in Year 4 (still held).

**Stories 126–200 — The Incident:**
The specific incident that elevated Danny's security classification in Year 1 is revealed. The specific event of Year 4 — what he did, what it cost him — is revealed.

_What they reveal:_ The hardest truths of what Danny survived and participated in.
_What they do NOT reveal:_ Nothing is held after Story 200. By then, the complete picture is available.

**Stories 201+ — Integration:**
Flashbacks become less about revelation and more about deepening. The reader now knows the full story; flashbacks illuminate specific emotional connections between past events and present moments.

---

### FLASHBACK TECHNIQUE RULES

1. **Tense:** Past perfect for the transition into the flashback (_"There had been a morning in Year 2..."_), then clean simple past inside the flashback.

2. **Length:** Two to four paragraphs maximum. Usually two. The flashback is a window, not a room.

3. **Connection:** Every flashback must connect to the emotional reality of the current adventure. The connection is felt, never stated.

4. **What is described:** Action. What Danny did, saw, heard. Never internal commentary on it. Never "I was ashamed of what I saw." Only "I watched it and then walked back to my cell."

5. **No exploitation:** The violence and difficulty of prison is never used for cheap gravity. It is rendered with the same calm precision Danny brings to everything.

6. **Daniel:** Daniel appears in the prison flashbacks only as absence and as visits. He is the thing Danny was missing, always. His presence in the flashbacks is felt through his non-presence.
